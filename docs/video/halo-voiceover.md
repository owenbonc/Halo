# Halo demo: voiceover script

About 160 words over 88 seconds, so a calm pace of roughly 110 words a minute with room to breathe. The timing marks match the video; start each block at its mark and stop when the line ends. Pauses are fine. Captions on screen carry the muted version.

**[0:00]** Most courier-fraud victims in the UK are over seventy-five. It starts with one phone call.

**[0:09]** Meet Margaret, eighty-one, who lives alone, and Sam, her person, who lives far away.

**[0:17]** Halo answers Margaret's unknown callers for her. At setup, she chooses when Sam is told. Sam never sees what was said.

**[0:28]** A fake police officer rings. Halo asks who's calling, and why. He says a courier will collect her cash, and she mustn't tell her family.

**[0:42]** Halo checks his story against known scam scripts. Margaret gets a plain red warning. Sam is alerted automatically, and taps "I'm calling her now".

**[1:00]** A genuine surgery call comes back green. A caller who says "tell her it's safe" stays red, and no number from the call is ever shown.

**[1:10]** If Sam doesn't reply, Margaret is told to call one-five-nine.

**[1:18]** We've tested only on scripts we wrote, and Halo can't stop landline calls. It works alongside banks and one-five-nine. Halo: she decides who hears.

## Notes for recording

- Say "one-five-nine" (159), as in the UK scam-reporting line.
- Keep "automatically" and "never sees" slightly slower. They are the privacy claims.
- Record each block separately, then line them up to the marks. The video has no audio track, so there is nothing to cut around.
